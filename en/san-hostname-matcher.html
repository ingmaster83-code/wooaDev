Certificate SAN & Hostname Match Checker

Enter a certificate's SAN (Subject Alternative Name) list and the hostnames you connect to and get a browser-like verdict. A wildcard covers one label only, IDNs compare as punycode and IPs match IP SANs only.

Example

✅ 6 of 9 match

Input — Certificate SAN / CN entries (one per line; IPs bare or with IP: prefix)

*.example.com
example.com
IP:203.0.113.10
api.internal.example.org
xn--bcher-kva.example

Input — Hostnames to check (one per line)

www.example.com
example.com
a.b.example.com
*.example.com
EXAMPLE.COM.
203.0.113.10
api.internal.example.org
bücher.example
foo.com

Result — Match results

Host | Result | Why
www.example.com | ✅ | matched by *.example.com
example.com | ✅ | matched by example.com
a.b.example.com | ❌ | * covers one label only (*.example.com does not match a.b.example.com)
*.example.com | ❌ | A wildcard is not a valid hostname (it belongs in the certificate only)
EXAMPLE.COM. | ✅ | matched by example.com
203.0.113.10 | ✅ | matched by IP:203.0.113.10
api.internal.example.org | ✅ | matched by api.internal.example.org
bücher.example | ✅ | matched by xn--bcher-kva.example
foo.com | ❌ | No SAN matches

Result — Notes

Modern browsers and RFC 9110 / 9525 ignore the CN and use SANs only; names only in the CN fail
Unicode hostnames are compared as punycode (xn--); a trailing dot is ignored

What this tool does

When you see 'certificate name does not match', find out immediately which name is missing.

  • *.example.com matches www.example.com but not a.b.example.com or the apex example.com
  • Case and a trailing dot are ignored; Unicode domains are converted to xn-- form before comparing
  • IP addresses match IP SANs only, not DNS SANs (IPv6 notation is normalised)
  • SAN checks: partial wildcards (w*.example.com), overly broad wildcards (*.com) and wildcards under public suffixes
  • Optional loose rule (* spanning labels) for some old clients

💡 Tips

  • To serve both the apex and www put example.com and *.example.com in the SAN list.
  • Modern browsers ignore the CN and use SANs only; a name that exists only in the CN fails.
  • A wildcard certificate widens the damage of a key leak; listing only the needed names is an option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a *.example.com certificate cover example.com?

No. A wildcard does not include the apex, so add example.com as its own SAN.

How do I see the SANs with openssl?

Run openssl x509 -in cert.pem -noout -ext subjectAltName.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
