What this tool does

For international meetings, maintenance notices and log analysis, working out "what time is that over there?" gets tedious. Enter a time and a source zone and get a table of results.

  • Specify the input zone and target zones by IANA name (Asia/Seoul, Europe/London …)
  • Daylight-saving rules come from your browser's time-zone data (Intl), correct for each year
  • Detects times that don't exist (spring) or occur twice (autumn) because of DST
  • Shows day changes (+1d / -1d) and the weekday
  • An explicit offset such as +09:00 in the input wins; an empty input means "now"

💡 Tips

  • When sharing a meeting time, list UTC plus a few attendee cities to avoid confusion.
  • Use region/city names like Asia/Seoul rather than "UTC+9" so daylight saving is applied correctly.
  • Leave the input empty to convert the current moment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are times on a DST change day interpreted?

A skipped time in spring (e.g. New York 2026-03-08 02:30) doesn't exist, so the earlier offset is used with a warning; a repeated autumn time is read as its first occurrence and flagged.

Doesn't Korea use daylight saving?

Not currently — South Korea stays at UTC+9 all year, while the US and Europe change offsets during the year.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
