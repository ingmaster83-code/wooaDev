Jenkins Cron Generator & Explainer (H syntax)

Turns a Jenkins build-trigger cron into plain language, shows the minute and hour the H (hash) symbol picks for your job name, and lists the next run times.

Example

✅ Valid Jenkins cron expression

Input — Jenkins cron expression (min hour dom mon dow, H allowed)

H(0-29)/10 9-18 * * 1-5

Result — Interpretation

Summary: every MON–FRI 9–18h · every 10 min (0, 10, 20)
Minute: 0, 10, 20
Hour: 9–18
Day of month: every day
Month: every month
Day of week: MON–FRI
About H: H lets Jenkins pick one fixed value per job within the range. The times above are an illustration for the job name "my-job" and may differ from what Jenkins actually chooses.

What this tool does

The cron you put in Jenkins Build periodically or Poll SCM looks like Unix cron but adds the H (hash) symbol, so the same line fires at different times for different jobs.

  • H is replaced by one value derived from the job name, which keeps every job from firing at minute 0
  • Ranged and stepped forms such as H(0-29)/10 and H(0-7) work, and aliases @hourly, @daily, @midnight, @weekly, @monthly are understood
  • When both day-of-month and day-of-week are set, either matching fires the job (OR) like Vixie cron
  • Change the job name to see which minute H picks, and change the display time zone to compare next runs
  • Anything that is not five fields, or out of range, is reported with the offending field

💡 Tips

  • Use H instead of 0 to avoid the top-of-the-hour pile-up, for example H * * * * runs at a different minute for each job.
  • Jenkins runs on the controller's time zone. Check whether it is UTC (you can also set it with a TZ= prefix).
  • Frequent SCM polling stresses the repository. Prefer webhooks and keep polling around H/15.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly does H pick?

It deterministically picks one value inside the range from a hash of the job name. The same job always gets the same time while different jobs are spread out. This tool computes a spread value from the job name you enter.

Can I use six fields?

Jenkins accepts only five fields: minute, hour, day, month, weekday. Use the Quartz cron generator if you need seconds.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
