What this tool does

C#'s DateTime.Ticks counts 100-nanosecond intervals since 0001-01-01. When an 18–19 digit number like 63xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx shows up in a log, database or serialized value, turn it into a date here.

  • Ticks → UTC ISO 8601 (7 fractional digits), Unix seconds/milliseconds, hex
  • Date → ticks with full 100 ns precision (BigInt arithmetic, impossible with doubles)
  • Also shows ToBinary() for DateTime.Kind=Utc
  • Explains when a value is outside the valid range (0 – 3155378975999999999)
  • Mix numbers and dates, one per line: numbers become dates, dates become ticks

💡 Tips

  • The ticks of the Unix epoch are 621355968000000000. Subtract it and divide by 10,000 for Unix milliseconds.
  • Reading ticks as a JavaScript Number corrupts the last digits — this tool uses BigInt.
  • DateTimeOffset ticks are local wall-clock based, not UTC, so check the offset separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is one tick?

1 tick = 100 ns = 0.0000001 s. One second is 10,000,000 ticks and one millisecond is 10,000 ticks.

What are the ticks of DateTime.MaxValue?

3155378975999999999, i.e. 9999-12-31T23:59:59.9999999 — the same bound this tool checks.
