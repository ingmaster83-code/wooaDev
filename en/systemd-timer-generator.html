What this tool does

systemd timers give you journald logs and dependency handling compared with cron, but the OnCalendar syntax is unfamiliar. Paste an expression to see what it means.

  • Aliases: minutely, hourly, daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly
  • Format: [weekday] year-month-day hour:minute:second with ranges (..), lists (,), steps (/) and the * wildcard
  • Shows the explanation, the normalized form (like systemd-analyze calendar) and the next N run times
  • Generates unit files with Persistent=true (catch up on missed runs) and RandomizedDelaySec (avoid pile-ups)
  • Give a service name and ExecStart command to get the .service file too

💡 Tips

  • Add Persistent=true to backups on machines that may be powered off.
  • If many servers call the same service at once, spread them with RandomizedDelaySec.
  • Enable with systemctl enable --now name.timer and verify with systemctl list-timers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is better than cron?

Results land in journald, services get dependencies, limits and retries, missed runs can be replayed (Persistent) and monotonic timers such as N minutes after boot are available.

OnCalendar vs OnBootSec?

OnCalendar follows wall-clock times (daily at 3), while OnBootSec and OnUnitActiveSec count elapsed time after boot or the last run. This tool handles calendar expressions.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
