What this tool does

Check a certificate installed on a server or audit it before it expires. Certificates never leave your browser.

  • Input: several PEM blocks (bundle), DER as hex or Base64, or a file (.pem .crt .cer .der)
  • Fields: subject and issuer, serial, not before / after with days left, signature algorithm, public key (RSA bits, EC curve), version
  • Extensions: SAN (DNS, IP, email), Basic Constraints, Key Usage, EKU, SKI and AKI, CRL and AIA (OCSP) locations, policies (DV, OV, EV), CT SCTs
  • Fingerprints: SHA-256 and SHA-1, equal to openssl x509 -fingerprint
  • Checks: expired, expiring soon, not yet valid, weak signatures (MD5, SHA-1), short RSA keys, missing SAN, hostname match, custom reference time

💡 Tips

  • Set a reference time to see ahead of time what will have expired.
  • Modern browsers match hostnames against the SAN only; a domain only in the CN fails.
  • A wildcard *.example.com covers one label below and not example.com itself or a.b.example.com.

Frequently Asked Questions

A private key was pasted too.

The tool decodes only the certificate and ignores the key but warns you; never share private keys.

What is the SHA-256 fingerprint used for?

To identify a certificate, for pinning and log matching; it equals openssl x509 -noout -fingerprint -sha256.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
