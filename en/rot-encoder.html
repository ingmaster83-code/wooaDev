What this tool does

Simple substitution ciphers aren't for security — they're for hiding spoilers, puzzles, CTF warm-ups and Easter eggs. The common ones are covered here.

  • ROT13: shift letters by 13 (the same operation encrypts and decrypts). ROT47: shifts all 94 printable ASCII characters
  • ROT5: digits only; ROT18: ROT13 + ROT5
  • Caesar cipher: encrypt/decrypt with a 1–25 shift; "Try all 25 shifts" cracks it when the shift is unknown
  • Atbash: mirror the alphabet (A↔Z)
  • Morse code: encode/decode letters, digits and punctuation (words separated by /)

None of these is cryptographically secure — don't use them to protect secrets.

💡 Tips

  • For a Caesar cipher with an unknown shift, use "Try all 25 shifts" and look for the readable line.
  • When decoding Morse, separate letters by spaces and words by / or three spaces.
  • Applying ROT13 twice returns the original text.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ROT13 secure?

No. Anyone can reverse it instantly — it's only for light obscuring. Use modern encryption such as AES for anything sensitive.

Does it work on non-Latin text?

ROT13, Caesar and Atbash change only A–Z letters, ROT5 only digits; other scripts are left unchanged. ROT47 also shifts ASCII symbols but keeps non-ASCII text.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
