DKIM & BIMI Record Parser

What this tool does

DKIM signs mail and publishes the public key in DNS (selector._domainkey.domain). A key that is too short or a malformed record makes verification fail.

  • Split quoted TXT strings are joined automatically
  • p is decoded as a DER public key to read the RSA bit size and exponent, or the 32-byte Ed25519 length
  • Errors under 1024 bits, advice to replace 1024-bit keys, 2048-bit recommended
  • Warns about revoked keys (empty p=), t=y testing mode, t=s strict mode, sha1 hashes and unknown tags
  • BIMI: checks the https SVG logo (l=) and certificate (a=) of a v=BIMI1 record and builds a default._bimi.domain record

💡 Tips

  • A 2048-bit key exceeds 255 characters, so check whether your DNS panel needs the string split.
  • To rotate, publish the new key under a new selector, switch your sending setup, then revoke the old selector with an empty p=.
  • BIMI only works when DMARC is quarantine / reject at pct=100, and most providers also demand a VMC / CMC certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I find the selector?

In the DKIM-Signature header of a received message: s= is the selector and d= the domain. The DNS name is s._domainkey.d.

Are 1024-bit keys still acceptable?

RFC 8301 allows them only as a minimum. Some receivers warn or reject, so move to 2048-bit.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
