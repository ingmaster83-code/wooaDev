What this tool does

WHOIS or an ISP gives you ranges like "1.2.3.0 - 1.2.3.255", but firewalls and cloud consoles often accept only CIDR.

  • Cuts the range into the largest aligned blocks first, yielding the minimum number of CIDRs
  • An aligned range becomes one block (10.0.0.0 - 10.0.3.255 → 10.0.0.0/22); misaligned ones split into several
  • Enter a CIDR to see its start/end addresses and size; IPv6 ranges work too
  • Errors (start > end, mixed IPv4/IPv6, bad format) are explained per line

Frequently Asked Questions

Why several blocks instead of one?

A CIDR block has a power-of-two size and must start at a multiple of that size. A range that doesn't fit this needs several blocks to be expressed exactly.

What about 0.0.0.0 - 255.255.255.255?

It is the single block 0.0.0.0/0.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
