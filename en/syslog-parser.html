Syslog Message Parser (RFC 3164 & RFC 5424)

Splits traditional messages like <34>Oct 16 09:12:03 web01 sshd[2412]: … and RFC 5424 messages like <165>1 2026-10-16T… into PRI (facility, severity), time, host, app, process ID, structured data and message.

What this tool does

Decode device and Linux logs into something readable and move them to JSON.

  • PRI: <number> = facility × 8 + severity, shown as a facility name (auth, local4 …) and severity (emerg … debug)
  • RFC 3164: Mon day h:m:s host tag[PID]: message (no year or zone, so your year and UTC are assumed and marked with ?)
  • RFC 5424: version, ISO 8601 time (offsets to UTC), host, app, PROCID, MSGID, structured data and message
  • emerg, alert, crit and err are highlighted with 🚨 and a severity count is summarised
  • JSON output of the parsed result

💡 Tips

  • RFC 3164 logs can get the wrong year around New Year, so take care near that date.
  • When merging logs from many servers prefer the 5424 format and UTC.
  • In rsyslog use the RSYSLOG_SyslogProtocol23Format template for RFC 5424 output.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I read the PRI number?

Integer division by 8 gives the facility and the remainder the severity: 34 → 4 (auth), 2 (crit).

Does it read journald output?

Formats such as journalctl -o short-iso differ and are not supported; the target is syslog wire format (3164 / 5424).

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
