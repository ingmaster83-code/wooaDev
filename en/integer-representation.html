What this tool does

Computers store negative integers as two's complement almost everywhere. You no longer have to turn -42 into 1101 0110 by hand.

  • Integer → bits: two's complement, one's complement, sign-magnitude and hex at the chosen width (4–64 bits)
  • Bits → integer: 0b… / 0x… input read as both signed and unsigned values
  • Shows representable ranges (e.g. 8-bit −128…127 and 0…255) and explains out-of-range input
  • Flags that the most negative value (−128) has no one's-complement or sign-magnitude form
  • Pairs well with the Bitwise Calculator for mask checks

💡 Tips

  • To negate in two's complement: invert all bits (one's complement) and add 1.
  • −1 is all ones (1111 1111 / 0xFF in 8 bits).
  • To read a string of 0s and 1s as a bit pattern rather than a decimal number, choose the bit-string option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why two's complement?

The same adder circuit works for signed and unsigned numbers, there is only one zero (no +0/−0), and the range gains one extra value.

Are one's complement and sign-magnitude still used?

Rarely for integers, but the ideas live on in checksums (the Internet checksum is a one's-complement sum) and in the sign bit of floating-point numbers.

Is my data uploaded to a server?
