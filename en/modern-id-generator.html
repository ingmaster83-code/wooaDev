What this tool does

UUID v4 is long (36 characters) and unsortable. Depending on the use, several alternatives are better.

  • Nano ID: 21 characters of A-Za-z0-9_- by default; choose length and alphabet, with the entropy in bits shown to judge collision risk; unbiased selection via rejection sampling
  • KSUID: 4-byte timestamp (seconds) + 16 random bytes as 27 Base62 characters, sortable by time; generate and decode
  • UUID v7: 48-bit Unix millisecond time + randomness in standard UUID form / TypeID: prefix_Base32(UUIDv7), e.g. user_01h455vb4pex5vsknk084sn02q
  • Everything uses crypto.getRandomValues — not the predictable Math.random

💡 Tips

  • For short public URLs, 10–12 Nano ID characters are often enough; check the entropy figure for collision risk.
  • To avoid look-alikes, remove 0O1lI from the alphabet.
  • KSUID time has second resolution; UUID v7 and ULID use milliseconds.

Frequently Asked Questions

How safe is a Nano ID?

The default 21 characters from a 64-symbol alphabet give about 126 bits of entropy — comparable to UUID v4's 122. Shorter lengths or smaller alphabets reduce it, so watch the displayed value.

Why is there no CUID2?

CUID2 requires SHA-3, which the standard browser crypto API doesn't offer, so output could deviate from the spec. It is left out on purpose.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
