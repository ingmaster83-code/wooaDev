What this tool does

If a cell's format is "General", a date shows up as a number such as 45292 — which you meet when exporting to CSV or reading the file from another system.

  • Integer part = date (1900-01-01 is 1), fractional part = time of day (0.5 = noon)
  • 1900 system reproduces Excel's old leap-year bug (60 = the non-existent 1900-02-29)
  • 1904 system: legacy Mac Excel basis (0 = 1904-01-01), selectable in workbook options
  • Enter dates/times to convert back; handles many lines
  • Supported range: 1900-01-01 (1) to 9999-12-31 (2958465)

💡 Tips

  • Excel and Google Sheets share serials from 1900-03-01 (61) onward but may differ by a day before that.
  • If you don't know a file's system, convert the same number in both — they differ by 1,462 days.
  • If you don't need the time, use only the integer part.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is serial 60 special?

For Lotus 1-2-3 compatibility Excel treats 1900 as a leap year and numbers a Feb 29 that never existed. So 60 doesn't map to a real date.

How far apart are the 1900 and 1904 systems?

The same serial points to dates 1,462 days (4 years + 1 day) apart, which is why dates shift when workbooks using different systems are combined.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
